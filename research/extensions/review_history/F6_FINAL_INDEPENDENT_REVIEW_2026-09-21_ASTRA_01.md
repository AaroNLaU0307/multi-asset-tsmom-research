Per-family and leave-one-family-out diagnostics have no promotion or rescue power. There is no post-result family selection in the adopted design.

### F. Fifteen calendar-year blocks — acceptable with limitation

There is no universal block-count threshold that makes 15 automatically invalid. Every year contains events, and the final metadata records comparable event counts (27–32), rather than an effect identified solely by one treated cluster. Resampling complete years preserves joint within-year event/control dependence; P2 is refitted on each resample, using the same year draws as P1. The target remains the event-weighted mean, not an equally weighted average of annual means.

Nevertheless, 15 is a small number of resampling units. Cluster asymptotics depend on the number of clusters; a large observation count does not substitute for many clusters. Pairs-cluster methods can have poor small-sample behavior, and the literature gives no universal safe cutoff. [Cameron and Miller, sections II.F and VI](https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/Cameron_Miller_JHR_2015_February.pdf).

My admissibility judgment is specific to the capped historical, dependent claim. The percentile interval is **nominal/approximate**, not established finite-sample 95% coverage. Entire calendar years are not made independent or exchangeable merely by being complete: material multi-year dependence, regime shifts, leverage or tail concentration can compromise the approximation. LOYO checks sign sensitivity only; it does not validate bootstrap coverage, exclude two-year dependence, or establish stable magnitudes. More bootstrap draws improve numerical resolution, not the amount of historical information. No alternative block scheme was compared and no power or precision was estimated from returns.
